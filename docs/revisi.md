# revisi — after the editor's decision

*[Baca dalam bahasa Indonesia](id/revisi.md)*

**v1.3.0** · [download zip](https://github.com/nulis-not-just-writing/skills/raw/main/dist/revisi-1.3.0.zip)

Handles the revision after an editorial decision: breaking reviewer comments into trackable items,
deciding which to act on and which to decline, writing the response letter, and checking it is
complete before it goes back.

## What it is actually for

### 1. The comments arrive and you do not know where to start

> *"Here is the editor's decision and comments from two reviewers. Help me build the revision."*

Comments are broken into individually trackable items rather than read as a wall of paragraphs.
Each item gets a decision: act on it, or decline it with a reason. The response letter is then
built from those decisions.

### 2. Two reviewers contradict each other

The most paralysing situation, and the one most often resolved the wrong way: by counting votes.

**Decisions follow criteria, not distribution.** Two reviewers who agree do not automatically beat
one reviewer with the stronger argument. What is weighed is the argument.

### 3. You want to decline a request without sounding defensive

**Symmetry of the burden of proof** applies: the burden for declining is exactly as heavy as the
burden for accepting. "It is a lot of work" is not a reason; neither is "I felt awkward saying no".

And **register is independent of severity** — declining a major request does not call for more
pleading language, and accepting a minor one does not license a dismissive tone.

### The most expensive failure at this stage

**A response letter claiming changes that were never actually made.** The editor will go looking
for them in the manuscript and will not find them. Claims are written only for changes that exist.

The companion rule matters just as much: the editor must be able to **find** the change. Give a
page, a line, or a section name — not "this has been corrected".

## When to use it

- You received *major revision*, *minor revision*, or *reject and resubmit*
- You are unsure how to answer a reviewer comment
- You want to decline a reviewer's request without sounding defensive
- **Two reviewers contradict each other** and you do not know which to follow
- You want to check the response letter is complete before sending

## Three principles that keep decisions honest

**Symmetry of the burden of proof.** The burden for *declining* a reviewer's request is as heavy
as the burden for *accepting* it. Declining because it is inconvenient is not a reason; accepting
out of deference is not either.

**Decisions follow criteria, not distribution.** Two reviewers agreeing does not automatically
outweigh one whose argument is stronger. The argument is what is judged, not the headcount.

**Register is independent of severity.** Declining a major request does not require more pleading
language, and accepting a minor one does not license dismissiveness.

## When a reviewer demands the RQ change

That is **structural** work, not text revision. Go back to `nulis` in audit mode before the
response letter is written — answering a structural comment with a patched paragraph shows.
